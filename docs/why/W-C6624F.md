## What was asked

The owner asked for a better engine panel, which got a bar showing who is better. The decision was
made then. This record was written afterwards, from the code.

## What was done and why

The engine's score in centipawns is turned into a chance of winning with the curve Lichess
publishes, `2 / (1 + exp(-0.00368208 × cp)) − 1`, and the bar shows that chance. Lichess first
clamps the score to ±1000 centipawns. This code does not, which changes the bar by less than a
few percent of its length at the ends. A pawn up in an
equal position moves the bar a lot, and a pawn more when a rook ahead hardly moves it, which matches
how much the pawn changes the result. A mate fills the bar.

## Alternatives rejected

- A straight scale of pawns, cut off at a fixed number: a pawn would move the bar the same amount in
  every position, and every won position would look alike past the cut-off.

## Assumptions

- The constant was fitted to Lichess games, which are mostly faster than the games in books. It is
  used as it is, without a fit of our own.

## When to revisit

If Stockfish's own win-chance estimate (UCI `WDL`) is used in the panel, the bar could show that
instead.
